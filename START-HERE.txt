Four folders. Read this page, then go to the one you need.
To BUILD the whole thing step by step, open IMPLEMENTATION-GUIDE.md.


1-firmware/            the code that goes on the boards
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  node_sender/
      ESP32 + ultrasonic sensor. This is the board that goes out to
      the water. Solar and battery, no internet, no SIM.
      Nothing in this file needs editing - every setting is entered
      later from its own web page at 192.168.4.1.

  node_receiver_8266_cloud/           <-- USE THIS ONE
      ESP8266 + LoRa + siren relay + LED. Sits at your house on
      mains power and your WiFi.
      Keeps its own hotspot at 192.168.4.1 AND uploads to your
      website at the same time.
      FILL IN the block at the top: your WiFi name, your WiFi
      password, your domain.

  node_receiver_8266_offline/
      The same receiver without the uploading. Use it if you have
      no website yet, or no internet where the receiver sits.
      Everything local still works: the dashboard, the LED, the
      siren.

  1-firmware/txt/ holds the same three sketches as plain .txt files,
  for sharing or pasting. The .ino files are the originals.

  Each sketch sits in a folder with the same name as the file. The
  Arduino IDE requires that - do not move the .ino files out of
  their folders or the IDE will refuse to open them.


2-website/             the three files that go on your hosting
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  index.html     the whole site: start screen with Client and
                 Administrator sign-in, client dashboard, admin board
  admin.html     only a redirect to index.html#admin
  api.php        the entire backend, one file
  .htaccess      keeps the database out of the browser. DO NOT SKIP.
  README.txt     full instructions - upload, SSL, MySQL, how clients
                 sign in, what the admin portal shows and does not

  Upload all four into public_html.
  Then open your site IMMEDIATELY and create your administrator
  on the Administrator card,
  before anyone else can.

  The database creates itself on first use. There is no fifth file
  to install.

4-older-versions/      superseded, kept only so nothing is lost
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Earlier sketches from the build: the first bench test, the ESP32-C3
  receiver before we moved to the ESP8266, the tank-only versions, and
  the very first node and gateway drafts.

  You do not need any of these. They are here in case you want to look
  back at how something worked at an earlier stage.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SHORTEST POSSIBLE PATH
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Install the Arduino IDE, both board packages, and the LoRa
     library by Sandeep Mistry.
  2. Wire and flash the sender. Set the tank height at 192.168.4.1.
  3. Wire and flash the receiver, offline version first.
  4. Bench test on your desk: move a book under the sensor, watch the
     number change at 192.168.4.1. Do not go further until this works.
  5. Buy the domain and hosting. Upload the four website files.
  6. Create your administrator on the Administrator card of the start screen.
  7. Reflash the receiver with the cloud version, filling in your WiFi
     and your domain.
  8. Read the key off 192.168.4.1, activate it on the website, choose
     a PIN, write down the client ID you are given.
  9. Install it outdoors.

  Steps 1 to 4 need no internet, no domain and no money beyond the
  parts. Do them first.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE THING WORTH REMEMBERING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Sender -> air -> receiver -> your router -> your domain -> any phone.

  Only one hop in that chain needs internet, and it is the one sitting
  safely on a roof rather than standing in a flood. If the internet
  dies, the local page, the LED and the siren all keep working - the
  siren decides on the receiver itself, for exactly this reason.
